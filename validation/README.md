# Validation by simulation

The tests and intervals of the audit were checked on simulated data where the right answer
is known. The results describe these simulated scenarios only; the data were generated by the
author's own models, and real datasets can differ from them in ways that change the results. Covered: the shortcut tests (both nulls), the missingness-mechanism tests (CRT,
patient group swap, the two-test rule, including the case where the age field is the target), the repeated-CV intervals of balanced accuracy and
gain, and the group-bootstrap intervals of Cramer's V and of the complete-case SMD. Not
simulated: the interval of the normalized gain (the gain interval divided by 1 - null mean),
the `p_naive` columns (labelled as not valid for clustered data) and the bootstrap intervals
of the value-association table (same bootstrap as Cramer's V).

`validate.py` uses a smaller gradient-boosting model (30 rounds) than the audit (100). This should
not change the false-positive rate of the permutation tests, but it can change power.

Simulated data (unless stated otherwise): 200 patients with 1-8 images each (one lesion per
image, as in ISIC 2020), 85% of a patient's labels alike, 30% of patients mostly in the
second class, a camera that is constant within a patient, age per patient. SLICE-3D style
scenarios: 200-300 patients with 25-100 tiles each, malignant tiles rare (about 1%), the
tests run on every malignant tile plus up to 20 random benign tiles per patient, and the nulls
re-apply that rule to the permuted labels.

## False-positive rate (no real effect; nominal 5%)

With 19 permutations a valid test rejects at p <= 0.05 at most 5% of the time (exactly 5%
without ties). The Monte Carlo standard error of a rate near 5% is about 1.5 points with 200
replications and about 2 points with 100-150, so differences of a few points are within noise.

| Test | Scenario (`validate.py` scenario) | Replications | Rejected at 5% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shortcut test, lesion null (not used as the test when lesions sit inside patients) | camera unrelated to the label (`shortcut_nested_null`, column `p_perm_unit`) | 200 | 14.0% |
| Shortcut test, size-matched patient null (the test there) | same runs (`p_perm`) | 200 | 5.0% |
| Shortcut test, lesion null (the test when groups are lesions) | lesion groups with 1-3 images (`shortcut_lesion_groups_null`) | 200 | 4.5% |
| Shortcut test, patient null | 8 classes, three rare with 8-15 patients (`shortcut_rare_classes_null`) | 200 | 6.5% |
| Shortcut test, patient null re-applying the subsample | SLICE-3D style (`shortcut_subsample_null`) | 150 | 2.0% |
| Missingness: CRT | missingness decided per patient by age, unrelated to the label (`mech_null`) | 120 | 15.8% |
| Missingness: patient group swap | same runs | 120 | 5.0% |
| Missingness: claim (both tests reject) | same runs | 120 | 4.2% |
| Missingness: claim, subsample re-applied | SLICE-3D style, missingness by age only (`mech_subsample_null`) | 100 | 0.0% (not informative: model (b) never used the label, gain 0 in every run) |

## Power (real effect present)

| Test | Scenario | Replications | Detected at 5% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shortcut test, patient null | camera reveals the class for 25% of patients (`shortcut_nested_power`) | 200 | 48% |
| Shortcut test, patient null re-applying the subsample | SLICE-3D style, camera raises the malignancy risk 0.004 -> 0.03 (`shortcut_subsample_power`) | 100 | 100% |
| Same test without re-applying the subsample (for comparison) | same runs | 100 | 0% (the null equalled the observed score in every run) |
| Missingness claim (both tests) | missing probability +0.25 for the second class (`mech_power`) | 120 | 78% (CRT alone 89%) |
| Missingness claim, subsample re-applied | SLICE-3D style, a patient with a malignant tile lacks the field with probability 0.8 instead of 0.2 (`mech_subsample_power`) | 100 | 27% (CRT alone 86%) |

The patient null and the two-test rule cost power; in these simulations that was the price
of keeping the false-positive rate near 5% when labels cluster within patients. A field that is
not reported as label-dependent may still be. On a SLICE-3D style subsample the group swap has low power:
patients only swap with patients of exactly the same number of tiles (about 60% of the tiles
could move here), and the label adds little to a model that already sees patient-level
inputs. The `group_swap_movable_share` and `group_movable_share` columns report how much
could move in a real run.

## Missingness of the age field itself (`age_target.py`)

The patient swap holds the patient's age band fixed. When the age field is itself the target,
that band is left out (a patient without any age would otherwise sit in an "na" band that equals
the target, and the swap could not test anything). These runs check the test in that case, with
a confounder: age is missing for whole patients more often in men, and men are more often in the
second class. 250 patients with 1-4 images, labels alike within a patient, the full
`missingness_mechanism` path, 19 draws per test, 100 replications (Monte Carlo standard error
about 2-2.5 points near 5-10%).

| Scenario | CRT | Patient swap | Claim (both tests) |
|---|---|---|---|
| null: missingness depends on sex only | 11% | 10% | 7% |
| power: missing probability also +0.25 in the second class | 63% | 50% | 47% |

With a confounder of this kind both tests reject more often than 5% under the null, and the
two-test rule only partly corrects it (7%, about one Monte Carlo standard error above 5%). A
label-dependence claim for a field whose missingness also depends on label-related fields
should therefore be read with this in mind. Before the age band was left out, the patient swap
could not detect label-dependent missingness of age at all (see
`tests/test_mechanism.py::test_group_swap_works_when_age_is_the_target`).

## Coverage of 95% intervals

| Interval | Replications | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Balanced accuracy, repeated CV with corrected variance (Nadeau and Bengio) (`gain_ci`) | 200 | 94.0% |
| Gain over the null, same | 200 | 94.0% |
| Bias-corrected Cramer's V, group bootstrap (missing indicator vs label) (`coverage.py`) | 200 | 95.5% |
| Complete-case SMD, label, complete vs all (basic bootstrap) | 200 | 93.0% |
| Complete-case SMD, age, complete vs all (percentile bootstrap) | 200 | 95.0% |
| Complete-case SMD, age, complete vs rest | 200 | 94.0% |
| Complete-case SMD, label, complete vs rest | 200 | 94.5% |
| Complete-case SMD, acquisition (true value about 0), complete vs all | 200 | 94.5% |

The bootstrap intervals use 1000 resamples, as in the audit. For the CV intervals the target is the mean of the estimates over the replications (the
expected CV performance at this sample size); for Cramer's V and the SMD it is the value in
one sample of 200,000 patients. With 200 replications a coverage estimate has a Monte Carlo
standard error of about 1.5 points. The corrected repeated-CV variance has no exact theory
behind it (no unbiased estimator of the CV variance exists; Bengio and Grandvalet 2004), so its
coverage is known only from simulations like these.
